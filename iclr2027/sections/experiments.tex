\section{Experiments}
\vspace{-0.05in}
We evaluate PDCM on two mixtures from the \textbf{Nemo-Gym} post-training suite~\citep{nvidia-nemotron-post-training-v3} and \textbf{Reasoning-Gym}~\citep{reasoninggym}.

\paragraph{Nemo-Gym} mixture focuses on fundamental agentic abilities, code generation, instruction following and tool calling, on three datasets. We incorporate four datasets. \textbf{\texttt{code\_gen}} contains competitive coding challenges from OpenCodeReasoning~\citep{ahmad2025opencodereasoningadvancingdatadistillation} dataset. \textbf{\texttt{IF}} is an instruction following dataset based on the WildChat-1M dataset~\citep{zhao2024wildchat1mchatgptinteraction} with instructions from the Open-Instruct code base~\citep{ahmad2025opencodeinstructlargescaleinstructiontuning}. \textbf{\texttt{xlam\_fc}}~\citep{zhang2024xlamfamilylargeaction} contains single-turn tool-calling data in xLAM format. \textbf{\texttt{workplace}}~\citep{nemogym}  tests multi-step tool-calling abilities in a harness that simulates real-life logistics tasks in an office.

\paragraph{Reasoning-Gym} mixture evaluates reasoning capabilities through script-generated games. Our experiments include six varieties. 
\textbf{\texttt{ARC-1D}} is an abstract pattern-induction puzzle where the agent infers the grid transformation rule from examples.
\textbf{\texttt{countdown}} is a numbers game in which the agent combines given numbers and fixed set of operations to hit a target.
\textbf{\texttt{zebra}} is a logic game where you use clues and deduction to match attributes across different colored houses.
\textbf{\texttt{word\_sorting}} is an alphabetical word-sorting task.
In \textbf{\texttt{puzzle24}}, one needs to combine four numbers and basic arithmetic operations to make 24. 
In \textbf{\texttt{manipulate\_matrix}}, one must apply given grid operations  to reach the final state.

We include more details on the datasets in Appendix~\ref{app:data}.

\paragraph{Experimental Setup.} The main experiments are run using Qwen3-4B, with additional experiments in Qwen3-8B. All experiments are run in \texttt{/nothink} mode. The main experiments are run on two H200 with a training budget of 48 hours, unless otherwise noted. All experiments are implemented with \texttt{VeRL}.
For the Nemo-Gym mixture, PDCM and Aioli control the allocation quota of each dataset. On Reasoning-Gym, we find that averaging $\widehat U_{t,k}$ over difficulty levels erase
the policy improvement signal PDCM relies on. We
therefore let the allocation methods control each difficulty level directly, so a $K$-task
Reasoning-Gym mixture is effectively a $3K$-unit allocation problem.
\vspace{-0.3cm}
\paragraph{Baselines.} All methods share the model, RL algorithm, data pools, and training budget, and differ only in
how the per-step mixture is chosen or how the resulting updates are combined.

\textbf{Uniform} is a multi-task baseline that fixes the mixture at $u=1/K$ for the whole run.

\textbf{Single} trains one specialist per task: $K$ separate runs, each with $1/K$ of
multi-task data budget.

\textbf{PCGrad}~\citep{pcgrad2020} holds the mixture uniform and intervenes on the update
instead, projecting conflicting task gradients onto one another's normal plane. It represents
the gradient-surgery family.

\textbf{Aioli}~\citep{chen2025aioli} is the closest prior allocation method. Like PDCM it
adjusts the mixture online, but it does so through the parametric mixing law.

\vspace{-0.05in}
\subsection{Main Results}
\vspace{-0.05in}

\Cref{tab:main} reports the performance of PDCM and baseline on the Nemo-Gym mix and a three task mixture on a three-task mixture that includes \texttt{ARC-1D}, \texttt{countdown} and \texttt{zebra}, abbreviated as \texttt{ACZ}. PDCM attains the highest total on all four suite-metric combinations, leading the uniform
mixture by $6.3\%$ and $5.3\%$ on Nemo-Gym (pass@1 and pass@4) and by $2.4\%$ and $1.3\%$ on
ACZ; Aioli is the runner-up on Nemo-Gym and the uniform mixture on ACZ.
Scoring each method task by task against the uniform allocation, PDCM's largest single-task shortfall
across the $14$ task-metric cells is $1.5$ points, against $6.9$ for Aioli, $11.2$ for
Single, and $37.5$ for PCGrad. PDCM falls below uniform in $3$ of $14$ cells and never by more than
that $1.5$ points, whereas PCGrad does so in $8$ and Single in $10$. Leading on aggregate while
staying level with uniform on every individual task is the behavior the constraint~\ref{eq:constraint_upper_bound} is meant to
produce, and it demonstrates PDCM's superior ability to maintain even development from the other multi-task methods. We report the corresponding validation curves in Fig.~\ref{fig:mix4_curves}.

\vspace{-0.1in}
\subsection{Allocation Dynamics}
\label{sec:dynamics}
\vspace{-0.05in}

We study the allocation dynamics of PDCM on the 4-task Nemo-Gym mixture. As shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:dynamics}, the allocation can be roughly broken down into three phases. Through the first $200$ steps $\texttt{code\_gen}$ is most often top-funded, induced by its large improvements early in training. Between steps $200$ and $300$ leadership passes to $\texttt{xlam}$, whose measured improvement degrades when it is under-allocated, which sustained its continual allocation.  Once $\texttt{xlam}$'s policy improvement was able to grow without further allocation, $\texttt{workplace}$ becomes the best-funded task for the remainder of the run around step 300.


Three of the four tasks receive a dominant phase. The
exception, $\texttt{IF}$'s proportion oscillates between $0.15$ and $0.45$, and these spurts of
budget are enough for it to maintain lead against the mixed baseline. Across all
four, the \emph{time-averaged} allocation remains nearly uniform (every task
within $\pm0.01$ of $1/K$). The near-uniform mean indirectly support that the
no-task-left-behind constraint of \Cref{eq:constraint_uniform_baseline} takes effect. A remarkable side effect of such allocation is that PDCM induces larger learning gradient from each batch, as seen in \Cref{fig:gradnorm}.

We scale PDCM control from the 9 tasks in the ACZ mixture in the main result to 18 tasks with the additional \texttt{word\_sorting}, \texttt{puzzle24} and \texttt{manipulate\_matrix}. \Cref{fig:scaling} shows PDCM consistently improves baseline performance with the increase number of components.

\subsection{Ablation}
\vspace{-0.05in}
\input{iclr2027/fig/ablation_table}

We ablate the effect of constraint~\ref{eq:constraint_uniform_baseline} in two ways. First, we remove the constraint entirely, reducing it to an unconstrained utility maximization problem, which is known to be efficiently solved by online stochastic mirror descent (OSMD)~\citep{audibert2013regretonlinecombinatorialoptimization}. Second, as a soft ablation, we remove the dual control $\lambda$, yielding $\mathrm{PDCM}_{-\lambda}$, where the task-wise score $s_t = G_t^T \mathbf{1}$ is solely driven by the curvature. We compare both settings to the full PDCM and the uniform baseline in \Cref{tab:lambda-ablation}. We can see that both ablations under-perform compared to the full PDCM.

However, the primal curvature also carries enough information that allows $\mathrm{PDCM}_{-\lambda}$ to outperform the baseline.
